# CRM - REAL-WORLD READINESS REVIEW

## Verdict
The current CRM is a thin sales toy, not a revenue operating system. It can list deals, contacts, and companies, and it can move cards around a pipeline, but that is the floor. Real companies need account structure, activity history, automation, governance, and forecasting depth.

## What Exists Today
- Basic deals, contacts, companies, and pipeline movement.
- Minimal scoring and pipeline management.
- Simple modals and forms for record creation.
- Enough UI to look like a CRM, not enough logic to run one.

## What Is Missing
- Account hierarchy for parent-child companies, subsidiaries, and groups.
- Activity timeline that unifies calls, emails, meetings, notes, tasks, and attachments.
- Custom objects for industries with real complexity.
- Duplicate detection, merge workflows, and data hygiene rules.
- Territory management, ownership rules, round-robin assignment, and queue logic.
- Forecasting by stage, close date, probability, and rep.
- Playbooks, call scripts, next-step enforcement, and sales methodology support.
- Permission layers for sensitive fields, deal value, margin, and forecast visibility.
- Import/export tooling that can move from HubSpot, Salesforce, or spreadsheets.

## Why It Fails in Real Companies
- A CRM is not just storage for contacts. It is the system that should tell sales leadership what will close, what is at risk, and what actions need to happen next.
- The current build has no meaningful operating discipline. There is no evidence of SLAs, approval gates, audit trails, or pipeline governance.
- Without activity intelligence, managers cannot coach reps or trust forecasts.

## Real-World Requirements
- One account view per business entity with hierarchy and ownership.
- A canonical timeline for every customer and prospect.
- Contact roles on deals and companies.
- Stage exit criteria, required fields, and locked transitions.
- Forecast categories, commit tracking, and pipeline hygiene alerts.
- Lead-to-account matching and conversion logic.
- Revenue team dashboards by manager, segment, and region.

## Templates Needed
- Pipeline templates by sales motion.
- Account plans by segment.
- Discovery call and qualification templates.
- Loss reason templates.
- Forecast review templates.
- Renewal and expansion templates.

## Automation Ideas
- Auto-create follow-up tasks when a deal stage changes.
- Alert when a high-value deal goes stale.
- Route accounts by territory or segment.
- Trigger onboarding tasks when a deal is won.
- Recalculate forecast risk daily.
- Flag duplicate accounts and contacts before save.

## Fix Strategy
1. Build the activity timeline and account hierarchy.
2. Add routing, scoring, and deduplication.
3. Add pipeline governance and forecast controls.
4. Add templates, playbooks, and manager dashboards.
5. Add import/export paths for real migration work.

## Done Means
- Sales leadership can trust the forecast.
- Reps can manage every account in one place.
- The CRM can survive a real migration and real audits.

# ACCOUNTING - REAL-WORLD READINESS REVIEW

## Verdict
Accounting is extremely far from production grade. If the platform cannot produce trustworthy financial statements, period close, reconciliation, and audit trails, it cannot claim business readiness.

## What Exists Today
- Accounting pages and some financial surface area.
- Basic links to the ERP concept.
- No evidence of a complete ledger and close process.

## What Is Missing
- Chart of accounts management.
- General ledger postings.
- Journal entry workflows and approvals.
- Accounts receivable and accounts payable aging.
- Bank feeds and reconciliation.
- Period close controls and lock dates.
- Financial statements: P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow.
- Tax reporting and jurisdiction handling.
- Audit trail for every financial mutation.

## Why It Fails in Real Companies
- Finance requires trust, not just screens.
- A broken reconciliation process destroys confidence fast.
- Without period close controls, anyone can rewrite history.

## Real-World Requirements
- Double-entry accounting discipline.
- Posted vs draft transaction states.
- Exportable ledgers and audit evidence.
- Multi-entity, multi-currency support where needed.
- Transaction source mapping from invoicing, purchasing, inventory, and banking.

## Templates Needed
- Journal entry templates.
- Month-end close checklists.
- Reconciliation templates.
- Financial pack templates.
- Tax filing templates.

## Automation Ideas
- Post approved invoices to the ledger.
- Match bank feed entries to open items.
- Flag unusual journals and variances.
- Freeze closed periods automatically.
- Send close task reminders.
- Generate scheduled financial statements.

## Fix Strategy
1. Build the ledger and posting model properly.
2. Add AR, AP, bank feeds, and reconciliation.
3. Add close controls and audit logs.
4. Add financial statement generation.
5. Connect finance to every upstream module.

## Done Means
- Finance can close the books without spreadsheets as the real system.
- Reports reconcile to the ledger.
- Historical data cannot be casually rewritten.
